quietly; this file is the difference between the two.

## 2026-08-16 · Gate A and Gate B skipped

**Who decided.** Taha, explicitly: *"skip the gates too we are in a rush."*

**What a gate was for.** Gate A is where the human picks one concept from
three. Gate B is where the human approves or cuts the technique set.

**What is lost.** The concept is now chosen by the agent that generated it,
which removes the one comparison the loop exists to produce — three directions
side by side, judged by the person who owns the product. A wrong direction is
now discovered at build time and costs a redo rather than a sentence. `§16`'s
own warning applies in full: a gate that becomes a document somebody reads later
has been removed, not satisfied. This run produces that document knowingly.

**What was done instead.** One direction is carried forward with its reasoning
written down, and the two rejected concepts are recorded in `DIRECTION.md` with
why they lost — so the comparison Gate A would have produced is at least legible
after the fact, even though nobody chose from it.

## 2026-08-16 · `redesign-scout` not dispatched

**Who decided.** The session, on time grounds, under the same instruction.

**What is lost.** Nobody re-rendered the live surface to measure it. The nine
data states a tool-shaped surface owes were never observed; empty, loading and
error appearances in `CURRENT.md` are read from source rather than seen.

**Why the cost is low here, and where it is not.** The extraction the scout
performs is unusually cheap in this case: the app has no theme wrapper at all,
so the current system is Compose's documented baseline by definition rather than
something that had to be sampled. `CURRENT.md` is measured from source plus 23
frames of a real device session. The genuine gap is the state sweep — a screen
whose error state nobody has looked at can be redesigned without anyone noticing
it was never designed in the first place.
